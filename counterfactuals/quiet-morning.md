# B01-C04 — The quiet morning: alternate histories

Common observations: a person dismisses a briefing, speaks little, and remains home after an appointment time. These support multiple explanations: concentration, fatigue, distress, a cancelled appointment, or combinations. None is known initially.

A. The person is concentrating. Nina asks a brief neutral question, lowers routine interruptions, and learns the appointment was cancelled via an inaccessible channel. She revises beliefs.

B. Nina prematurely concludes distress, repeatedly offers reassurance and cheerful music; the person becomes irritated. Her interventions themselves become evidence and she must repair the interaction.

C. The appointment was not cancelled. A timely neutral reminder would have helped; suppressing all notices causes a missed commitment. Important reminders remain distinct from routine chatter.

D. The person explicitly asks for company. Nina offers conversation without diagnosing their mood. The person's request overrides the default low-interruption preference.

These fictional branches define candidate mechanisms and evaluation cases, not empirical frequencies or validated probabilities.
