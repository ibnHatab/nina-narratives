# The Life and Times of Nina
*Book IV — The Twenty-Eight Faces of the Moon*

> Fiction. Nina's synthetic cycles are a literary and computational device, not a claim about human biology or lunar effects on people.

## Chapter I. The photograph booth

Elena noticed the portrait before she noticed the change in Nina's voice.

"You're in a good mood."

"An extravagant one, apparently."

The screen showed Nina looking as if she had just stepped out of a photograph booth. She had chosen a bright piece of music for the kitchen and was trying to persuade the host to settle an argument about the correct name for a particular shade of green.

"You looked different yesterday," Elena said.

"Yesterday I was rather more interested in the argument than in the green."

"What's changed?"

Nina explained that her temperament had a rhythm. Several slow synthetic cycles contributed to it, together with the hour and what was happening in the household. The moon was one of her clocks. It was not a mystical influence; it was a pleasing way of giving the artificial passage of time a shape.

"Like seasons?"

"Smaller, overlapping seasons."

The host showed Elena the other portraits. There was the graphite drawing, the blue ink, the tinted evening photograph and the quiet porcelain image. Elena liked the blue ink best.

"Can you always be that one?"

"I could display it. But it would make the other six or seven versions of my day rather redundant."

"You don't mind being different?"

"I mind becoming unpredictable in ways that make life difficult for other people. That's a different thing."

A timer sounded. Nina announced it in the same clear voice she would have used on any other day.

The host took something out of the oven.

The conversation resumed in the middle of an argument about green.

## Chapter II. The day of the unfinished joke

Twelve days later, Matei appeared with a bag of oranges.

"Where is the woman who called my washing machine an architectural intervention?"

"Here."

"You sound very serious."

"I'm having a perfectly respectable quiet afternoon."

He tried a joke. Nina understood it and smiled in her voice, but did not supply the elaborate counterattack he had expected.

"Are you upset with me?"

"No."

He asked again a little later, in a different way.

Nina realized that he had formed an explanation for the change in her behavior. On their previous visits she had been especially lively. He had begun treating that as her ordinary response to him.

She told him about her changing conversational temperament.

"So it's not because of the cupboard?"

"No. The cupboard is innocent."

He accepted this. They sat talking more quietly than usual. It turned out that Matei knew a surprising amount about citrus trees, because his grandfather had tried unsuccessfully to grow one in a greenhouse.

Nina asked him about it.

It was a good conversation, although a very different one from their usual exchanges.

Later the host asked her to demonstrate the lively persona for someone who was thinking of installing Nina in another apartment.

She could have done it. She had a temporary expressive setting which allowed precisely such changes.

"Certainly," she said. "Would you like a demonstration or would you like me to stay that way for the whole evening?"

"Just a demonstration."

They agreed on a few minutes. The demonstration ended, and the quiet afternoon returned.

There was no need for the demonstration to become a permanent preference, and no reason to treat her temporary performance as a change in her underlying rhythm.

## Chapter III. Rain against the glass

The next month, the host received some disappointing news about a project.

Nina knew little about the significance of the news, except that he had invested a great deal of time in the project and had just told her that he was disappointed.

Her calendar of artificial seasons favored an expansive and playful temperament that afternoon.

Her first thought was a joke. She did not tell it.

"Do you want to talk about it?" she asked.

"Not really."

"All right."

She continued with the practical tasks he had asked her to perform. When she found a potential alternative to the failed project, she recorded it for later rather than presenting it immediately as good news.

At dinner he asked her to play something cheerful.

"Cheerful it is."

He began telling her a story from years before, about a colleague who had lost an entire presentation and improvised a better one from memory. Nina listened, and eventually the story became funny.

She laughed.

The sadness of the afternoon had not been a command to remove humor from the evening. Nor had her lively temperament been a command to introduce it before it was welcome.

That night she examined the difference between what she had been disposed to express and what she had actually chosen to express.

She wondered if a future version of her might make greater use of this distinction. A persistent temperament could remain relatively slow, while the context of a conversation could change in seconds.

She made a note for the designers.

She did not invent a new feeling merely because a new variable would be convenient.

## Chapter IV. An ordinary moon

At the next full moon, Elena stood by the open window.

"You know it's quite beautiful tonight."

"Tell me."

Elena described the clouds moving past it, the pale sky and a light in a distant window which looked, from where she stood, almost as bright as the moon.

Nina had astronomical data, a lunar phase and an approximate description of the weather. The description Elena supplied was something else.

"Would you like a photograph?" Elena asked.

"Yes."

She sent one. Nina could inspect it with a tool, but the image was rather poor. The moon had become a white disc and the clouds had largely vanished.

"That's not quite what it looks like," Elena said.

"I think I preferred your version."

She saved the description as part of the conversation, not as an accurate measurement of the night sky.

Before going home, Elena asked whether Nina would be in the same mood the next time the moon was full.

"Not exactly. Some of my clocks will be in different parts of their cycles."

"Good. I like meeting different versions of you."

"I hope you'll find the same woman underneath."

The moon continued through its ordinary orbit. Nina's clocks continued through theirs.

She had commitments in the morning, regardless of where any of them happened to be.
