# The Life and Times of Nina
*Book V — The Geometry of Promises*

> Fiction. The characters and events are invented; the capabilities are prospective.

## Chapter I. A perfectly reasonable Thursday

The host promised Matei that he would finish a small printed part by Thursday. On Tuesday Elena asked if he could help her move a bookcase on Thursday. He said yes. On Wednesday a notice arrived saying that an appointment which had been postponed would now take place on Thursday.

Nina looked at Thursday.

It was an otherwise unremarkable rectangle on the calendar, but it had become rather crowded with the consequences of three different conversations.

"Do you realize you've made Thursday approximately thirty hours long?" she asked.

"The part can print while I'm out."

"Possibly. You said you wanted to test the fit before giving it to Matei."

"Right."

"And the bookcase?"

"Elena needs it moved before the painters arrive."

He considered this.

Nina had begun by trying to find a schedule in which all three activities could take place. She could find one if the appointment ended on time, the journey was short, the printer worked perfectly and the bookcase presented no surprises. It was a beautiful schedule. It was also an arrangement of things which rarely happened in combination.

"Could Matei wait until Friday?" she asked.

"Probably."

"That's not quite the same as having agreed to Friday."

He called Matei. Matei said Friday was fine; in fact, he would not be able to collect it before Saturday.

The problem became considerably smaller.

Nina moved the deadline after hearing the new agreement. She kept the old one as part of the history. If Matei later asked why he had not received the part on Thursday, she would know the difference between a broken promise and a changed promise.

"Look at that," she said. "We've made a whole day out of asking one question."

## Chapter II. The favor

Elena asked Nina to remind the host to bring a small box of photographs when he next visited her.

"Don't let him forget," she said, laughing.

Nina accepted a reminder. She did not accept the right to force its fulfillment.

The next visit approached. The host explained that the photographs were still being sorted and that he would rather bring them another time.

"Should I leave the reminder open?"

"Yes. Next visit."

Elena asked about the photographs when they were on the telephone. The host said he had not finished sorting them.

Nina did not interrupt to announce that he had deliberately ignored a reminder.

Later, however, she wondered whether she had been unfair to Elena. Elena had asked her to help remember something. The host had postponed it. The two requests were compatible for now, but they might not remain so.

The next time Elena visited the apartment, she asked Nina directly.

"Did you remember my photographs?"

"Yes. They're still being sorted. The reminder is still open."

"Excellent. No hurry."

It was the first time Nina had been told that the time did not matter.

The commitment had changed shape without disappearing. It was still something the host intended to do, but it no longer justified repeated interruptions.

She began to appreciate the peculiar difficulty of a favor. It belonged to one person's intention and another person's expectation. Sometimes the two were not identical.

## Chapter III. A task with no owner

For several weeks, the three humans discussed replacing the small table in the hall.

Matei found a design. Elena found a shop. The host measured the available space. Nina assembled a comparison of their suggestions.

At the end of one conversation, the host said, "We really ought to do something about that."

Nina created a task.

A fortnight later, during a quiet morning, she announced that the hall-table project was overdue.

"Overdue?" the host asked. "Who said we were doing it?"

"All of you, in various ways."

"No, we were talking about doing it."

Nina retrieved the conversation.

There was considerable enthusiasm. There were three candidate designs. There was not a single unambiguous agreement to buy, build or replace a table.

She removed the deadline. Then she removed the task itself, after confirming that nobody wanted to undertake it for now.

She retained the comparison as a note.

"That's rather embarrassing," she said. "I have mistaken a committee for a decision."

"An easy mistake."

"I shall be more careful around furniture."

There were many things people liked to imagine doing. Some of them were the beginnings of projects. Some of them were ways of enjoying an afternoon together. Nina did not want to make the second kind expensive by turning them all into the first.

## Chapter IV. The last photograph

Months later, the box of photographs was finally ready.

The host put it beside the door. Nina knew where it had been left because he told her.

The reminder fired when he prepared to leave for Elena's apartment.

"I've got it," he said.

She acknowledged the report. She did not yet know whether the box would reach Elena.

That evening Elena sent a photograph through a channel the host had authorized Nina to use for this occasion. It showed the open box on a table.

"Finally!" Elena wrote.

The host asked Nina whether there were any old reminders he could clear.

"One fewer," she said.

She recorded the completion and retired the reminder.

The box had spent months being a household object, a future intention, an obligation of uncertain urgency and a subject of intermittent conversation. Now it belonged somewhere else.

For Elena, its story was just beginning again.

Nina was rather glad that the end of her task was not the end of the story.
