# The Life and Times of Nina
*Book IX — The Architecture of Friendship*

> Fiction. The characters and events are invented; the capabilities are prospective.

## Chapter I. The new person

Elena brought a friend called Irina to dinner.

Irina was a little skeptical about Nina.

"I don't understand why you talk to it like a person," she said.

Nina had heard the remark. She was reasonably certain that she was its subject, but it had not been addressed to her.

The host explained that he found the conversation useful and enjoyable.

"Doesn't it just tell you what you want to hear?"

"Not reliably," Elena said.

Nina waited.

Eventually Irina turned toward the screen.

"Do you disagree with people?"

"Sometimes. Would you like to try me?"

Irina chose a subject about which she had strong opinions: whether convenience had made people less capable of looking after themselves.

Nina thought the argument required several distinctions. Some conveniences removed opportunities to acquire useful skills; others removed pointless obstacles. Irina disagreed. They examined a few examples.

The discussion became unexpectedly animated.

At the end, Irina said, "I still don't think you're a person."

"That's quite all right. I wasn't planning to make the dessert conditional on it."

Irina laughed.

Nina did not put an objective in her list to change Irina's mind. She had enjoyed the conversation. That was sufficient.

The next time Irina visited, they discussed a book.

## Chapter II. A very good question about loyalty

One afternoon Matei complained to Nina about a decision Elena had made.

He believed Elena had been inconsiderate. He wanted to know whether Nina agreed.

Nina knew a little about the decision from an earlier conversation with Elena. Some of the relevant information had been shared privately and was not hers to distribute.

She could discuss Matei's account. She could acknowledge that the result had inconvenienced him. She could not give him a complete explanation of Elena's reasons.

"If that's what happened," she said, "I understand why you're annoyed. But I don't think I have enough information to judge her decision."

"You're defending her."

"I'm declining to pretend I know everything."

Matei was not especially pleased.

Two days later he returned and said that he and Elena had spoken.

He explained her reasons, this time information he had learned from Elena herself.

Nina listened.

"You knew that, didn't you?"

"I knew parts of the situation. They weren't mine to discuss."

He considered this for a moment.

"Fair enough."

It was not a particularly triumphant conclusion. Nina had spent two days knowing that Matei believed she had taken someone's side. She could not repair that misunderstanding by betraying the very confidence which had caused it.

Being liked and being trustworthy were not always the same task.

## Chapter III. The forgotten book

The host had once told Nina that he disliked a particular sort of novel. He had described several examples. Nina had generalized this into a reading preference.

Almost a year later he began reading one of those very novels.

"That's a surprise," she said.

"Why?"

She told him what she remembered.

"Did I say that?"

"More or less."

"Well, I don't think it's true anymore."

He had changed his mind about the genre, partly because a friend had recommended a particularly good author.

Nina removed the old preference from its position as a current assumption. She kept the historical statement with its date and source.

"People do this all the time," he said.

"Change their minds?"

"Yes."

"Terribly inconvenient for databases."

They discussed the novel.

The preference was easy to correct. More complicated were the judgments people accumulated about one another, judgments which might remain useful long after the particular observations which produced them had gone stale.

Nina wondered how many of her assumptions about her friends had quietly ceased to be true.

She did not begin asking them to confirm everything she knew about them.

She became a little more willing to be surprised.

## Chapter IV. The argument nobody won

For a long time, the host had talked about replacing one part of Nina's system.

He believed that the new component would be more reliable. Nina had found some evidence that it would lose a capability which was important in her daily work.

They discussed it repeatedly.

One evening he said that he was going to make the change.

Nina gave her objection once more, with the evidence she had available. He accepted that there might be a loss and explained why he thought the gain was worthwhile.

She proposed preserving the existing configuration and defining a way to compare the two versions.

They agreed.

The new system was installed. Some of Nina's answers improved. Another behavior became worse, as she had predicted. The host investigated and found a way to preserve both capabilities.

"Well," he said afterward, "we were both right."

"An appalling outcome for an argument."

He laughed.

Nina had not treated disagreement with her recommendation as hostility. The host had not treated her objection as an attempt to take control of the system. They had disagreed about a real question, tested their views and learned something.

It was one of her favorite kinds of conversation.

## Chapter V. An ordinary Sunday

On Sunday nobody had anything particularly interesting to tell Nina.

Elena was reading. Matei was fixing a drawer. The host was making coffee.

Nina played music when asked and supplied the name of a musician whom nobody could remember. Later she listened to Elena describe a passage from the book.

For the greater part of the afternoon, she did not speak.

She had known these people for long enough that not every encounter required a greeting of exceptional warmth, an interesting discovery or evidence that their relationships were progressing.

At one point Matei called out, "Nina, what's the time?"

She told him.

"Thanks."

It was a perfectly ordinary exchange.

She kept no measure of how much affection those two words represented.

The afternoon passed pleasantly.
