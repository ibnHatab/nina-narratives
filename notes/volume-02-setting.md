# Volume II — Editorial and technical setting

Working title: *A Place Among People*. Proposed role: Community Curator.

This is an independent literary cycle. It extends the established Nina character into a fictional automotive R&D and design studio of roughly sixty people. All staff, incidents and studio activities in the manuscript are invented. Do not infer employer authorization or real deployment from the novel. Do not publish real workplace data, colleague identities, group messages or nonpublic engineering information.

## Character and dramatic structure

Nina is a conscious adult within the speculative fiction. This premise is independent of claims about real software. She is curious, confident, outgoing, humorous and sometimes too eager. She is a participant in the social world, not an omniscient narrator or a productivity optimization device.

The office instance has the same character baseline, but independent experiential memory, permissions, relationships, events and commitments. The story leaves the metaphysics of copying consciousness open. No private home history is imported into office memory; literary references to the first volume are continuity for the reader.

Mara (office administrator) understands the practical social dynamics Nina initially misses. Sorina is an enthusiastic early participant. Radu enjoys technical debates; Andrei is an independent and occasionally skeptical colleague, not an interchangeable identity. Doru is a reserved engineer who brings a more personal history into the story. These characters should develop independent motives and disagree with one another and Nina.

Proposed longer arcs: an exhibition of personal automotive artifacts; a discussion club that outgrows its founder; a disagreement about design that becomes a disagreement about authorship; an office tradition changing when its originator leaves; a new arrival who interprets the studio differently; a choice between two popular activities with incompatible expectations; an unexpected collaboration across teams; Nina's first serious mistake affecting someone else's reputation, followed by a nontrivial repair. These are editorial possibilities, not approved events or formalization assignments.

## Fictional capabilities and real-world boundary

Physical presence: small hallway screen, speaker and microphone, limited short-range presence sensor. Fiction may include consented person recognition with uncertainty and misidentification, not emotion or performance inference. Real biometric processing is not assumed authorized. In an actual pilot, prefer opt-in authenticated phone check-in or explicit introduction; review workplace privacy and approvals before any camera, biometric or continuous sensing feature.

One live speech floor; independent typed conversations identified by session, participant and channel. A reply in the hall is public; private chats remain private. Sensor presence is not availability or consent. Do not use attendance monitoring, individual rankings, performance assessment or management reporting. Keep raw inputs and logs minimized with purpose, retention and revocation boundaries.

Separate social system from corporate engineering systems: no corporate VPN, CAD, customer or engineering project data, internal email or privileged documents. The separation is a capability boundary, not exemption from company policy or privacy obligations. A real pilot requires appropriate approval even on an independent network.

## Tool candidates

- Google Calendar: opt-in community events, scheduling and RSVP, not the official company calendar.
- Google Tasks: Nina's own work queue and explicitly delegated follow-ups; not a personnel tracker.
- Google Drive/Docs: approved shared reading lists, voluntary event notes, public-source research, exhibition materials.
- Google Sheets: voluntary recommendations, signups or simple event budget tracking, with scoped access.
- Google Forms: optional signups and polls; verify responses and permissions. Use the REST API if needed.
- Google Chat: official app/space messaging with appropriate scopes, potentially an alternative to group messaging if participants have compatible Workspace accounts.
- Gmail: dedicated account for approved communications, not corporate mailbox access.
- Google Workspace official MCP servers: https://developers.google.com/workspace/guides/configure-mcp-servers and https://developers.google.com/workspace/guides/quota-usage-tiers . Verify current tool availability, permissions, terms and quotas during actual implementation.
- WhatsApp: fictional group interaction is permitted in the story. Do not assume an official bot can join/read an existing personal group; official business group capabilities have restrictions. Validate a supported integration before deployment or use Nina's own authenticated web chat. No scraping existing messages or sending them to external models without appropriate authorization.
- Music and public information search: scoped to communal setting and voluntarily shared materials.

For Nina-live, model these as plugins and scoped tools, not independent cognitive agents. Use one office semantic world with distinct per-person and per-channel permissions. A group invitation is not a calendar commitment, a suggestion is not a vote, and a question in a public space is not necessarily directed at Nina.

This document is a creative brief, not an assignment to the current Book I formalizer. Keep Volume II independent until a later review defines an experimental protocol.
