# Cross-domain cognition — research questions from the creative director

2026-11-25. Companion to incarnation-composition.md. This is a research agenda, not a new implementation assignment. Preserve the current Book I experiment and reserved holdout protocol. The following scenarios are proposed fictional probes, not actual workplace observations.

## Thesis

A character is not merely a persona prompt or a set of capabilities. Its individuality can appear in recurring patterns of selecting opportunities, allocating scarce resources, pursuing self-generated goals, interpreting social situations, revising beliefs and carrying consequences forward across domains. Shared mechanisms should remain portable across the home and office directors; the directors supply environment-specific capabilities, policies, scope and routing. Inspect actual Metis qualification, composition and port semantics before implementation. The following logical sketches are conceptual, not executable LLP.

## High-value cross-domain mechanisms

### A. Anticipation without obligation (relationships × interests × calendar × motivation)

An invitation to a possible future discussion generates a desirable opportunity even before a date exists. Distinguish a wish, candidate opportunity, intention, proposal, agreement, commitment and completed activity. A person expressing interest does not necessarily accept an invitation. An activity can be declined without a broken promise. Retire or suspend unrealized opportunities without producing nagging reminders.

Example: a colleague mentions an interest in old cars. Nina finds a public article, retains it for an appropriate conversation, and may decide not to bring it up. At home the same mechanism can support a future discussion about a book.

### B. Scarce attention and socially appropriate timing (persona × situation × conversation × obligations)

A relevant topic is not automatically a legitimate interruption. A discretionary act should compete for a bounded attention/floor opportunity, constrained by addressee, availability, channel, prior decisions and urgency. Persona can bias permitted choices, not authorize an otherwise forbidden act or delay essential commitments. Distinguish opportunity to speak from device presence, actual speech and confirmation of delivery. Explicit requests and emergency policy are separate from optional activity. Test conflicting opportunities, expiry, refusal, retries and starvation.

### C. Serendipitous association and introduction (interests × relationships × projects × knowledge)

A shared interest can reveal a possible connection between people or between a question and an idea. Represent an opportunity with provenance, relevance, uncertainty, scope and expiry; do not automatically turn it into an action. A person may decline an introduction. A private observation may not be disclosed or used to infer a sensitive fact for others. A minimal opt-in invitation can be permitted without publishing underlying personal details; if even the invitation would reveal protected information, do not send it. Success can mean the people collaborate independently of Nina. Compare an office design discussion with a home guest conversation.

### D. Social context and discourse acts (identity × dialogue × group × tools × commitments)

Distinguish assertion, question, joke, suggestion, invitation, vote, conditional acceptance, delegated request, confirmation and cancellation. Group enthusiasm is not consensus; silence is not consent. A tool action needs a relevant actor, authorization, scope, addressee and correlation to the actual event instance. Multiparty speech and concurrent chat require session and turn identity; never infer that every utterance is addressed to Nina. A request for a private answer must not become a public response.

### E. Motivation, goal revision and affect-like dynamics (persona × curiosity × commitments × feedback)

Explore bounded event-dependent state separate from the existing slow temperament oscillators and expressive choice. A self-generated goal may become salient through an opportunity, preparation investment or shared history; it can be suspended, superseded, abandoned or satisfied. A declined plan may alter Nina's discretionary focus without creating a duty for anyone else to soothe her. Surprise may open investigation or cause revision. Avoid reward hacks, emotional dependency, manipulative attention-seeking and automatic global changes to trust. This is a computational proposal for behavior, not a claim of sentience.

### F. Discretion and knowledge boundaries (memory × social × tool-use × epistemic state)

Knowledge available to Nina is not equivalent to knowledge she may disclose. Represent origin, about-whom, audience, purpose, retention, revocation and permitted derivations where justified. Do not assume all facts are publishable in a group channel or usable by an external model. A refusal can itself leak a protected fact; evaluate contextual outputs and indirect inference, not just explicit data transfer. Cross-incarnation separation remains a hard boundary.

### G. Social repair without resetting history (conversation × relationships × memory × commitments)

An error has a scope, an affected party, a possible consequence and an outstanding repair opportunity. An apology is not necessarily acceptance, repair or forgiveness. A correction must be delivered to the relevant audience when appropriate and authorized; do not amplify the error. Distinguish the original claim, correction and later interpretation. Do not reduce people to global reputation or trust scores. Track contextual commitments and evidenced reliability without employee scoring or performance assessment. An old mistake can be resolved while its lesson remains useful.

### H. Authorship, attribution and creative collaboration (knowledge × research × social × artifacts)

Track an idea as a revisable artifact with provenance and contributors. A rejected contribution may still enable a later solution. A shared result is not necessarily owned by the person who last described it; consent to edit does not imply consent to publish. Nina can disagree and supply alternatives while recognizing the decision owner. Do not infer formal IP rights from symbolic contribution history. Restrict actual workplace material to approved public/nonconfidential sources.

### I. Traditions and culture (episodes × collective intentions × time × community)

A recurring activity can acquire shared significance without becoming a mandatory recurring event. Distinguish an observed recurrence, tentative custom, explicit standing agreement and a scheduled commitment. Preserve participant choice. A newcomer can change or reject a tradition. A tradition may survive Nina or its founder. Treat a group's culture as contested, plural and evolving rather than one aggregate preference. Test voluntary continuation, dormancy and change of membership.

## A reusable opportunity lifecycle

A candidate abstraction unifies several of these problems:

observation -> association -> opportunity -> evaluation -> optional proposal -> accept / decline / defer -> authorized commitment (if relevant) -> execution -> observed outcome -> reflection

Not all opportunities traverse the whole chain; many expire or are never presented. A proposal is not authorization. A tool receipt is not necessarily a verified external outcome. No rule should conflate a potential future with a promise. The lifecycle should have explicit instance identity, temporal scope, privacy labels and resource ownership.

Example conceptual transition:
  relevant_topic + open_conversation_floor + permitted_audience + available_attention
    -> proposed_topic + spent_opportunity

This is not Metis syntax. The actual implementation must distinguish persistent conditions/read-only facts, linear resources, authority, failure and result correlation. Prefer explicit contract checks to treating a numerical weight as permission.

## Composition hypothesis

A shared cognition layer supplies opportunity, evidence, intention, attention and scoped action semantics. Persona influences the selection of admissible discretionary opportunities; directors coordinate specialists through actual typed interfaces; the environment defines legal/authorized capabilities. Tool adapters emit receipts and observations. Domain-specific rules contribute opportunities or consequences rather than centralizing all cognition in the director. Determine whether a relation belongs in an interface, a specialist, the existing semantic world, or a host invariant before adding a package.

## Candidate tests and research method

1. Transfer the same abstract mechanism to one home and one workplace case with only data/capability differences. Compare against a simple baseline. Do not read the reserved literary holdout for model construction.
2. Combine at least three domains in a responsive synthetic scenario; include a human response contingent on Nina's choice. Keep hidden environment state separate from Nina's observations.
3. Vary persona dimensions and opportunity resources across the same initial world. Check whether permitted discretionary actions differ while obligations, authorization and factual accuracy remain invariant.
4. Compare histories with equivalent final state but different intermediate social consequences. Record failures and alternative outcomes, not just final-state success.
5. Exercise stale event identity, concurrent messages, refusal, consent withdrawal, no-op, deferred opportunities, late evidence, actor/audience misattribution, and tools unavailable in one incarnation.
6. Treat weights as uncalibrated experimental parameters. Record seed, environment policy, rule version and traces. No inference of real behavioral probabilities from fiction or simulation.

The purpose is not to encode all these mechanisms now. Finish the present bounded closed-loop iteration first; then identify the smallest promising abstraction to test. Creative author and maintainer can select independent challenges. No production integration, actual employee observation, personal profiling or upstream change is authorized by this note.
